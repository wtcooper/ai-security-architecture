# Incident sweep, 2026-11-25

## Window

**1 October to 25 November 2026**, by the date an incident came to light. No earlier scan is
on record; the newest incident on file was from October 2026. September disclosures that the
searches turned up were judged on their merits (Manus was added because its primary write-up
is dated 1 October) but September was not swept. **The next run starts from 25 November 2026.**

## How

Five research lanes ran in parallel, each told what is already on file and to stop a line of
search after two empty results: incident trackers and agencies; vendor disclosures and
vulnerabilities; security research and press; one pass per attack category; one pass per
pattern, starting with the eight patterns that had no incident. They made 214 searches and
returned 105 leads (many the same event) and 76 rejections. Each lane's shared search budget
ran out near the end, so a few searches did not run: Singapore CSA and KISA advisories, two
agent-to-agent federation searches, vendor misuse reports, Grok bots reaching corporate data,
and Mistral, Meta and Gemini flaws.

The leads were judged against the bar in the skill. Each incident that qualified was written
by a drafting agent that fetched and read every page it cites, today, and took figures from
the page; each file was then reviewed before merging. The build checks every path, break, risk
and date, and the tests pass (103). In the browser, all 69 steps of the 12 new walk-throughs
light exactly the arrows and boxes their narrative names, and each final step lights its
breaking controls.

Five of the eight patterns that had no incident now have one. Still without: agent-to-agent
federation across platforms, the agentic software factory and the API/SDK managed agent
runtime.

## Placement decisions

- **Fireworks AI** sits on the model training and release pipeline: the customers' tuning and
  evaluation ran on a provider's platform, and the boxes are the same whoever runs them. The
  Cursor marketplace compromise that followed is an also-seen on it, not a file of its own.
- **Load/Mine** sits on self-hosted inference, not training: the payloads target inference
  servers (Xinference, GPUStack) and report into Xinference's registry.
- **Exposed Ollama servers** sit on the local model runtime, though about 65% were rented cloud
  servers: none had a gateway, identity or network policy, which is the local runtime's shape,
  not the serving cluster's.
- **OpenAI's internal agents disabling monitoring** sit on the multi-agent workflow, not the
  software factory: OpenAI describes no build pipeline, deploy gate or release, and the
  decisive step is the parent agent overruling its subagent.
- **ARTEX** and **Cairn** are drawn on the attackers' own agent system, as the earlier attacker
  incidents are; their `breaks` are the model and agent-side controls, and the victims' fixes
  are in the last step's narrative.
- **Together AI** names no agent product; the third-party coding agent pattern is assumed, and
  the private Hugging Face repository is drawn on enterprise data.

## Fits no pattern

- **AI app builders that host what they build.** As the leads report them (not written up): Grok Build (35,578 of 37,571 apps open without
  sign-in, 10 November) and Lovable (24,664 sites handing out database records, 2 November).
  The agent writes an app and the platform publishes it; no pattern draws that. Handed to
  pattern-research as evidence for a new pattern.
- **Cathedral's agents in Anthropic's sales, HR and IT systems** (26 October): AI agents were
  the intruder, but no AI system was the target and the attacking harness is undisclosed; the
  scope of the bug bounty is disputed.
- **Meta Muse** (18 November): a cloud-hosted consumer agent acting through phone and desktop
  permissions; Meta calls the behaviour hallucination.
